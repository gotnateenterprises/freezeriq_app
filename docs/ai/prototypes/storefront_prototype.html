Design notes — what changed & why it converts

A
Real deadline, zero pressure

The order-week strip states a fact ("ordering for the week of Nov 17 · order by Wed") — genuinely useful for meal planning. The countdown timer and deals popup are gone: pressure widgets read as pushy and fight a cozy brand. Facts create urgency; timers create anxiety.

was: CountdownBanner + DealsPopup interrupt on load
J
First visit: prove it fast, sell it gently

A new customer today scrolls through hero → brand story → how-it-works → testimonial wall before seeing a single product, with a popup and countdown firing on arrival. The redesigned first visit compresses trust into one screen-length: question-hook hero, 3-chip how-it-works, a $10 welcome offer (auto-applied — no code hunting), then products SECOND — badged with social proof ("Most loved · ★4.9") and a low-risk entry point ("Perfect first try" sampler). The not-ready visitor gets a soft menu-email capture instead of a popup, so even a bounce becomes a subscriber.

was: products 5 sections deep; DealsPopup + CountdownBanner on load; no first-order offer; no email capture
B
"Your usual" — the stickiest card on the page

Returning customers see their name and a one-tap reorder of what they actually buy. Order history exists; this is the single highest-converting element a food storefront can add. New visitors see a "most loved this month" card instead.

C
Cards answer the three questions in one glance

What's inside (real recipe names, not marketing copy), who it feeds, what it costs — then Add. One column on mobile, big thumb targets, category chips scroll horizontally. Serif names + warm gradients keep it feeling like a kitchen, not a warehouse.

D
The founder note is the brand

Female-centric done honestly: a real woman, a real reason ("my third baby arrived"), her face near her food. Trust for family-food purchases is personal, not corporate. One five-star quote with a specific detail ("asked for seconds") beats a wall of testimonials.

E
Thumb-zone cart, always

The dark bag bar never leaves the bottom edge — count, total, one tap to view. Above it, the free-delivery progress bar appears only after the first add: "You're $30 from free delivery" is goal-gradient psychology — the customer upsells herself to finish the bar.

was: MobileStickyCart exists but competes with popups and a sticky category bar
F
The serving toggle is honest merchandising

Serves 5 / Serves 2 on one card ("family night" / "date night") instead of two confusing catalog entries. Heat-times per meal ("bake 40 min") sell the real product: a calm weeknight. The single pairing suggestion ("Families also love Apple Crisp") is one warm invitation — never a carousel of pressure.

G
Upsell = "round out your week," capped small

Bag add-ons are low-cost sides ($9–18) framed as completing her plan, not spending more. Three items max, horizontal scroll, one tap. Combined with the free-delivery bar this raises order value while feeling like help.

was: single configured upsell fires only at checkout — the decision moment, worst timing
H
Loyalty shown before checkout, not after

"This order earns 257 points = $12.85 toward next time" makes the NEXT order part of this one's value. The points chip in the header (240 pts = $12) greets returning customers with a reason to buy again. The LoyaltyWidget exists — it just needs to move upstream.

K
Fundraiser page: from brochure to order engine

Today's buyer page ends in a mailto: link — the buyer types their own order into an email and the coordinator re-types it into the portal. The redesign is a real self-serve form (bundles → name → "who are you supporting?" → submit) that records the order instantly as a held fundraiser order — still zero online payment: the confirmation walks the buyer to the coordinator's Venmo/check, exactly as the flow is designed. Seller credit fuels the leaderboard; the share row turns every buyer into a promoter; and the tenant's founder note + menu-email link appear only AFTER the order, so discovery never competes with the campaign. Try it: add a bundle and place the order.

was: "Order" opens the buyer's email app; nothing recorded; coordinator double-enters
I
Confirmation is the retention page

Warm thank-you by name, three concrete next steps ("we'll text you when it's packed — bring a cooler bag"), points earned banner, and the referral card ("give a friend $10") at the exact moment of peak goodwill. This screen currently says thanks and stops; it should start the next order and the next customer.
