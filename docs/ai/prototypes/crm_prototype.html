Design notes

A
Needs-attention strip answers "what do I do today?"

Campaigns ending soon, held orders awaiting closeout (the money number you already compute), and stale leads — each links to its filtered view. The stat cards weren't wrong, they were passive; this is the same data made actionable.

was: 3 static count cards (Active / High Leads / Held Orders)
B
Stage filters match the real pipeline — including Closed

Leads / Onboarding / Active / Production / Closed mirror the statuses the API actually emits (fixes the COMPLETED-tab mismatch where Closed campaigns never surfaced). Search covers org, coordinator, and campaign names.

was: ALL / ACTIVE / LEAD / COMPLETED — 'Closed' matched none of them
C
Campaigns grouped by organization

Your current table shows four near-identical "Karen Seuring / Ag in the Classroom" rows. Grouping puts the relationship first: one collapsible org header with rollup totals, campaigns nested under it — duplicates become visible instead of noise. Actions are labeled text buttons (Close out, Invoice, Portal) instead of five mystery icons; Invoice is hidden on leads, and "goal not set" renders as a quiet prompt instead of "$0 No goal set."

was: one flat row per campaign; icon-only actions; placeholder leads mixed in as fake campaigns
D
Org profile leads with the relationship, not a form

Contact, tenure, and the two actions you actually take (email, new campaign) — the edit form moves behind an action instead of dominating the page.

E
Pipeline stepper replaces the status dropdown

The Lead → Closed journey is visible at a glance and doubles as a click-to-advance control. Same statuses the API validates today — purely a presentation change.

F
Campaign cards carry the settlement story

Active campaigns show sold/held/days-left plus every outward link (order page, portal, scoreboard, pickup sheet) in one place. Closed campaigns surface the frozen settlement_total with a one-click "Create invoice" prefilled from it — the wiring gap the closeout audit flagged.

was: closed campaigns look identical to open ones; settlement_total never displayed
G
Toolkit becomes a checklist with state

Info packet / marketing packet / payment instructions / first share — each shows done-or-waiting instead of being buttons you have to remember whether you pressed.

H
Customers: one list, two lenses

People (meal buyers, with spend and last order) and Organizations (fundraiser partners, with campaign rollups) are the same table with a segmented toggle — replacing the Customer CRM vs Fundraiser CRM naming collision. An org's row links straight to the same org profile the Fundraisers screen uses.

was: two sidebar items both called "CRM"; org data split across two different detail pages
L
Rebook pipeline — the renewal engine

Fundraising is seasonal: an org that raised money last fall is your warmest possible lead for next fall. A simple query (closed campaign 6–12 months ago, nothing scheduled since) surfaces them with a suggested ask of last-result +10%, and "Clone into wizard" prefills everything. Repeat orgs already raise +23% vs first-timers — this makes rebooking a one-click habit instead of a memory exercise.

build: one query over existing campaign data + wizard prefill — no schema, no new APIs
M
Inbound lead capture from your own public pages

Every scoreboard viewer and storefront buyer is a parent in some other group. A "Run a fundraiser for YOUR group" CTA on those pages writes straight into a Leads inbox (the BusinessLead model already exists). Your existing campaigns become your ad network.

build: one CTA + one form → existing BusinessLead table → this inbox
N
Campaign health score — save campaigns while they're alive

Pace vs goal + days since last order + coordinator engagement (shares/downloads are already tracked in CoordinatorActionEvent) = a simple rule-based at-risk flag. Today a flop is discovered at closeout, when it's too late. The at-risk card prescribes the intervention: boost message, check-in call, extend the date. Also shown inline on the dashboard rows ("⚠ at risk" / "on pace").

build: computed score from existing fields — no ML, no schema
J+
Bundle auto-suggest — the wizard already knows what sells

Step 2 sorts the bundle checklist by real sales history: "⭐ Fundraiser favorite" = top sellers in past campaigns, "🔥 Top seller" = storefront best-sellers, top picks pre-checked. Rebooks preselect exactly what that org sold last time. One group-by query over OrderItem — data that already exists.

was: alphabetical checklist; tenant guesses what a school group will actually buy
P
Automations — the CRM acts on its own

Weekly Monday digest to the tenant, automatic rebook nudges when an org enters the 6–12 month window, same-day at-risk alerts, goal-hit celebrations to coordinators (with a share prompt), and win-back emails to lapsed customers reusing the existing "We Miss You" template. All of it hangs off ONE new cron route — the same infrastructure the planned monthly "Top of Mind" coordinator email needs.

build: vercel.json cron + one /api/cron/growth route + Resend templates that already exist
O
Impact report + lifetime value — the artifacts that sell next year

At closeout, auto-generate a branded one-pager ("You raised $2,847 — 168 bundles, 41 families") the coordinator forwards to their school board — which is exactly who approves next year's fundraiser. Lifetime raised on the org profile tells you which partners deserve white-glove treatment. Benchmarks card turns your own history into coaching ("every 3 shares ≈ 1 order").

build: reuses the existing flyer/PDF generation + settlement data
